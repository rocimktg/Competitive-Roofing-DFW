Fence Services We Offer

Whether you need a full new fence, a few damaged boards replaced, or a gate that actually latches, we handle it as a standalone project or alongside roofing work.

Cedar Privacy Fences

Full privacy fence installation using quality cedar — not the thin, knot-heavy boards some crews use to cut costs. Properly spaced posts set in concrete, correctly fastened rails, and a clean, consistent line from end to end.

Fence Repair & Board Replacement

Storm damage, rot, and general wear rarely take out an entire fence at once — usually it's a section of boards, a leaning post, or a broken rail. We repair what's actually damaged instead of pushing a full replacement you don't need.

Gate Installation & Repair

A fence is only as good as its gate. We install and repair gates that actually latch and swing properly over time — correctly sized posts and hardware rated for the gate's weight, not undersized hinges that sag within a year.

Staining & Sealing

Cedar left untreated will silver and crack faster under Texas sun. We stain and seal new and existing fences to protect against UV damage and moisture, extending the life of the wood significantly.

Full Fence Replacement

When a fence is too far gone to repair — widespread rot, storm-damaged throughout, or simply at the end of its life — we tear out the old line and install a new one, matching height and style to what your neighborhood and HOA require.

Repair or Replace? Here's How We Think About It.

Same honest framework we use for roofing decisions — we'll tell you which makes sense for your fence, not just push the bigger job.

Repair is usually the right call when…

  • Damage is isolated to a few boards or one section
  • Posts are still solid and set at proper depth
  • The fence is under 10 years old and otherwise sound
  • A gate issue is hardware, not structural
  • Only one side took storm or wind damage

Replacement often makes more sense when…

  • Multiple posts are leaning or rotted at the base
  • Rot or storm damage is spread across the whole line
  • The fence is 15+ years old and showing widespread wear
  • Previous repairs have already patched the same sections
  • You're changing height or style, not just condition

Fence Questions

How long does a cedar fence typically last?

A well-built cedar fence, properly stained and maintained, typically lasts 15-20 years in DFW. Post life is usually the limiting factor — even good boards don't help much once posts start rotting at the base, which is why we focus on proper post depth and concrete setting up front.

How often should I stain or seal my fence?

Every 2-3 years is a reasonable baseline in North Texas, given the UV exposure and temperature swings. A fence that's never been sealed will silver, crack, and warp noticeably faster than one that's maintained on a regular cycle.

Do I need a permit for a new fence?

It depends on the city and the fence height — many DFW cities don't require a permit for standard privacy fences under a certain height, but some do, and HOA approval is a separate requirement from city permitting. We'll help you confirm what applies to your property before work starts.

Can you match my existing fence style?

In most cases, yes, for repairs and partial replacements. For board style, height, and cap details, we'll do our best to match what's already there so a repaired section doesn't stand out from the rest of the line.

Why did my fence blow down in the wind but my neighbor's didn't?

Almost always it comes down to post depth and concrete — fences with posts set too shallow or without proper concrete footings are far more likely to fail in DFW's wind events. When we install a fence, post setting is where we don't cut corners, even though it's the part nobody sees once the job is done.
