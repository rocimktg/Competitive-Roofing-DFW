How the Claims Process Works With Us

This is the same process whether you've never filed a claim before or you're already dealing with an adjuster who's lowballed the scope.

1

Free Storm Damage Inspection

Before you file anything, we inspect the roof and tell you honestly whether the damage is worth pursuing. We're not interested in encouraging a claim that isn't there.

2

We Document Everything

Photos, measurements, and a written scope covering every damaged component — not just the roof, but gutters, fascia, and siding if they're affected too. This becomes the backbone of your claim.

3

You File the Claim

You contact your insurance company and open the claim — we're not licensed to file it on your behalf, but we'll walk you through exactly what to say and what documentation to reference.

4

We Meet Your Adjuster On-Site

This is the step that matters most. We walk the roof with your adjuster, point out what they might miss from the ground, and make sure our documentation and theirs are talking about the same damage.

5

We Complete the Approved Work

Once the scope and payout are settled, we do the work to the approved specification — and if the adjuster's initial estimate missed something, we'll help you file a supplement rather than eating the shortfall.

ACV vs. RCV: Know What Your Policy Actually Pays

These two terms determine how much of your roof replacement your insurance actually covers — and a lot of homeowners don't find out the difference until claim time.

Replacement Cost Value (RCV)

  • Pays the full cost to replace your roof with new materials
  • No deduction for the roof's age or wear
  • Depreciation is recoverable once work is completed
  • Generally the better policy type to have
  • Usually comes with a higher premium

Actual Cash Value (ACV)

  • Pays replacement cost minus depreciation for age
  • Older roofs receive a smaller initial payout
  • You may owe more out of pocket to complete the job
  • Depreciation is often not recoverable
  • Usually comes with a lower premium

Not sure which one you have? Check your declarations page or call your agent and ask directly — it's worth knowing before a storm hits, not after. We can help you understand what your specific payout means for the scope of work once you have your claim numbers in hand.

Insurance Claim Questions

What's a supplement claim?

A supplement is an additional claim filed after the original scope is approved, when damage or cost gets discovered that wasn't in the initial estimate — for example, decking rot found during tear-off. Supplements are a normal, routine part of the process, not a red flag.

Do I pay anything upfront?

You're responsible for your deductible — that's set by your policy, not by us. We don't require payment upfront beyond what's typical for scheduling a job, and we'll be clear about the payment structure before any work begins.

Will filing a claim raise my insurance premium?

It can, depending on your insurer and claims history — this is a question worth asking your agent directly before you file, especially for borderline damage. We won't push you toward filing if the damage doesn't clearly justify it.

What if my claim gets denied?

A denial isn't always final. If we've documented clear, legitimate storm damage and a claim is denied or underpaid, we can provide our written scope and photos to support an appeal. We can't guarantee an outcome, but we won't leave you without documentation to work with.

Do I have to hire CRC if I file a claim?

No. The inspection and documentation are free with no obligation to hire us for the repair or replacement. We'd like to earn the work, but the claim is yours regardless of who ends up doing the job.
